A Harvard working paper by Fiona Chen and James Stratton covers 718 firms. Engineers at firms that adopted AI coding agents added about **30% more code**. Finished work did not measurably rise, and getting code through review took **49% longer**. It is a job-market paper with no completed peer review.

The finding fits the per-PR attribution tools now shipping: drafting got cheaper, and the human checkpoint became the constraint. For builders measuring agent ROI, count merged and shipped work and review latency, not lines or PRs opened. Agent investment that doesn't also reduce review load (risk-tiered review, verification tests) may not change throughput.

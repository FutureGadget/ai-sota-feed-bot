Monday's feed was almost entirely about the unglamorous work of making AI agents operable — not launching them, but evaluating, debugging, securing, and plumbing them. Anthropic anchored the day: the company published more detail on how Claude Code generates its own execution harnesses to coordinate the recently introduced Dynamic Workflows, while developers chewed over the new Agent SDK pricing (one widely-shared post argued the change is a win for Codex). Simon Willison surfaced an Axios piece on the political clashes that briefly knocked Anthropic's models offline — a reminder that the frontier labs now operate as much in Washington as in research.

The strongest technical signal came from agent evaluation and reliability. AWS shipped Strands Evals for automated failure detection and root-cause analysis, walking through how to turn an agent's structured output into categorized failures with causal chains, and separately published a build-it-yourself research agent on Bedrock AgentCore. LangChain and Fireworks fine-tuned an open model into a trace judge that matches frontier-model quality at roughly a hundredth of the cost. Alongside the eval push, security and governance ran hot: Google Cloud's new CISO laid out four lessons behind its AI Threat Defense, InfoQ published a practical cloud AI-governance guide for architects, and a Permit.io post made the sharp point that coding-agent sandboxes don't actually solve credential authorization.

The one piece of hard infrastructure news was Google's $1.5 billion commitment to expand its Jackson County, Alabama data center campus across 2026–2027 — the kind of substrate the whole agentic stack quietly runs on. Underneath the headlines, a long tail of early-stage projects kept filling in the agent-infrastructure layer: communication buses, AI-native messaging, off-server execution, and tooling to measure what all of it costs.

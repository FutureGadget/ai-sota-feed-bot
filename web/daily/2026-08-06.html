Pods as Workers, Not Agents: Rethinking the Deployment Unit for AI Agents on Kubernetes

infoq_ai_mlDetails

The kagent project argues against one Pod per agent, since agents are bursty, short-lived, spawn subagents, and often wait on human approval.

Playbook takeaway

ProblemAgents are bursty and short-lived, so provisioning a dedicated Pod, Service, and ServiceAccount per agent wastes cluster resources and doesn't match how agents actually run.

ApplySplit agent identity from Pod lifecycle: run a fixed pool of long-lived worker Pods and schedule many lightweight logical agents onto them, suspending and resuming as work arrives. Move identity, RBAC, and network policy to the logical-agent/tenant level instead of per-Pod.

ExpectedThe kagent project's worker-pool redesign lets far more agents share the same fixed Pod pool instead of scaling Pods one-to-one with agent count.

Cloudflare AI Search: give your agents a search engine for your data

cloudflare_blogDetails

Cloudflare's AI Search product lets builders point agents at their own files and websites without stitching together separate search primitives.

Playbook takeaway

ProblemStanding up retrieval yourself means separately wiring crawling, chunking, embeddings, a vector store, and reranking, then keeping all of it in sync as your data changes.

ApplyUse a managed AI-search service that auto-crawls, chunks with overlap, embeds, indexes, and reranks on its own (e.g. `wrangler ai-search instance create --source <url> --type web-crawler`), then query it directly or expose it as an MCP endpoint for your agent.

ExpectedOne managed pipeline replaces the crawling, chunking, embedding, vector-store, and reranking stack most teams hand-wire separately, with hybrid semantic-plus-keyword search and cited results built in.

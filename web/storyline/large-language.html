Latest change

Nov 19: a deception study tests label-free knowledge and persona directions as lie probes at 70B scale, aimed at the transfer failures of linear probes. A second paper measures hallucination in cyber-threat-intelligence tasks.

Builder action

Before adopting any of these detectors, replay your own traffic: measure the jailbreak screen's false-block rate on benign prompts, check that a calibrated abstention threshold holds its error rate on your data, and count the extra model calls each adds.

  • Do the Nov 19 knowledge and persona directions detect lies on models outside the study, and at what false-positive rate?
  • Does the contrastive-prototype jailbreak detector report false-positive rates on benign prompts and hold up against adaptive attacks?
  • Does stability-compensated threshold calibration keep its error guarantee when the deployed model or prompt distribution shifts?
  • Does KV-cache reuse across model updates hold beyond the DeepSeek-V4-Flash case study?
  • Do published unlearning or knowledge-editing methods still pass when probed without explicit entity names, as ShadowBench suggests they may not?
